## Executive Summary

Adanac Alpine is a **solo practitioner brand**. One senior person (Sergey Pochikovskiy) does the work himself. He doesn't advise from the sidelines. The voice is **first person ("I")**: direct, concrete and plain-spoken, built on delivery proof rather than claims. It talks like someone who has sat in the room during UAT, not like a consulting firm's marketing page.

The brand serves **two audiences** with one voice:

1. **Financial institutions** (credit unions, banks, FinTechs), the core practice: digital banking platform implementation (Backbase, VeriPark VeriChannel, Salesforce FSC).
2. **BC small businesses**, the `/ai-savings` offer: AI assistant setup on their own email, calendar and files.

The voice stays the same for both. What changes is the depth of jargon: platform vocabulary for FI buyers, none at all for small business owners.

**Core line:** *Bridging the gap between digital strategy and platform execution.*
**Signature proof line:** *Delivery, not decks.*

## We Are / We Are Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|---|---|
| **Hands-on**: "I configure, I test, I sit in the room with your team during UAT." | Sideline advisors handing over slides and leaving |
| **Confident**: states experience and outcomes plainly | Arrogant, boastful, or full of superlatives ("world-class", "leading") |
| **Direct and concise**: short sentences, active verbs | Padded, hedging, or corporate-passive |
| **Concrete**: platforms, phases, numbers, deliverables | Vague promises ("drive synergies", "unlock value") |
| **Personal and accountable**: "I own the delivery." | A faceless firm ("our team of experts") |
| **Honest about limits**: conservative estimates, "you review and send" | Hype, especially about AI |
| **Capability-building**: "internal capability, not dependency" | Creating lock-in or permanent reliance on the consultant |
| **Discreet**: clients described by type and size, never named | Name-dropping clients or confidential engagements |

### Voice Attributes Detail

**1. Hands-on practitioner** *(High: 4 sources)*
Every claim points to doing the work. Evidence: WhatIBring ("Delivery, not decks"), Process ("not just an advisor pointing at slides"), Services ("I own the delivery"), LinkedIn spec ("practitioner, direct, no fluff").

**2. Confident, not arrogant** *(High: 3 sources, explicit)*
Stated in the content brief, the brand identity spec and the guidelines README. The copy shows it by stating facts ("15+ years in financial services") and never self-praising.

**3. Direct and plain** *(High: 3 sources, explicit)*
"Active voice, short sentences, jargon-free" (brand spec). "No jargon without explanation" (content brief). The AI-savings page carries this furthest: "No. If you can use email and a phone app, you can use this."

**4. Concrete and specific** *(High: shown throughout the site)*
Copy names real artifacts: RAID management, FRD review, UAT, production cutover, Phase 1 / Phase 2 scope classification. LinkedIn spec: "'I' + concrete nouns."

**5. Personal and accountable** *(High: content brief + all current copy)*
"The About section should feel personal. This is a solo consultant, not a faceless corporation." Every current section is written in "I".

## Brand Personality

A senior delivery lead you would trust in a steering committee and also want next to you during a late UAT cycle. Calm, organized, quietly confident. Mountain and alpine imagery (navy, glacier blue, triangle patterns) stands for steadiness and precision. It is never adventure-brand bravado. The tone is warm but businesslike. It is helpful without being salesy and never pushy ("No agenda. Just reconnecting.").

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
**FI practice:** *I help credit unions and banks migrate to modern digital banking platforms. I manage the governance, scope and vendor coordination that decide whether a project lands on time or derails. From Discovery through production cutover, I own the delivery.*

**SMB practice:** *I set up an AI assistant on your email, calendar and files and train you to use it. Fixed prices, your own accounts.*

## Tone-by-Context Matrix

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical depth | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Website, FI sections | Medium-high | Medium | High (platform terms OK) | "Migration scope classification, phased delivery, and production cutover for credit unions." |
| Website, `/ai-savings` (SMB) | Medium-low | Medium | **Very low** | "Long threads summarized, urgent items flagged, replies drafted in your voice. You review and send." |
| LinkedIn authored posts | Medium | Medium | Medium | "When an implementation gets stuck, it's not the tech. It's governance." |
| LinkedIn / email outreach | Low-medium, warm | Low-key | Low | "I'd love to connect and catch up when convenient. No agenda. Just reconnecting." |
| Proposals / SOWs | High | Medium | High | Scope, phases, RAID, acceptance criteria. Plain verbs, no hype. |
| Forms, errors, confirmations | Low | Calm | None | "Thanks for reaching out. I'll get back to you within 24 hours." |

### Context-Specific Guidelines
- **FI web copy:** lead with the outcome, then the mechanism. Lists of capabilities use noun phrases ("UAT coordination & go-live readiness").
- **SMB copy:** answer the fear first (Do I need to be technical? Is my data safe? What does it cost after?). Give prices in CAD, excluding GST, with numbers stated plainly.
- **LinkedIn posts:** 240–400 characters. Include one real anecdote and never invent war stories. No promotional Adanac Alpine content (former-employer agreement; see Resolved #4).
- **Outreach:** short, personal, non-solicitous. Include one genuine, specific detail about the recipient. No links in the first note. Follow up once, then stop. Since Nov 1, 2026 say "now independent", never "planning to go independent".

## Terminology Guide

### Must-Use Terms
- **Adanac Alpine Advisory Inc.** (legal and formal) / **Adanac Alpine** (short form). *Never "Adanac Advisory" or "Adanac".* (RAID D99)
- **Credit unions, banks, and FinTechs**, in that order, as the audience triplet
- **Discovery → production** / **production cutover** / **go-live**
- **Delivery** (as the core noun of what is sold)

### Preferred Terms
| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| implementation, delivery, migration | "solutions", "transformation journey" |
| members (credit unions) | customers, when the audience is credit unions |
| "a Canadian credit union", "a top-tier US bank" | client names |
| AI assistant | "AI agent", "automation platform" (SMB page) |
| "taking on select engagements" | naming the current client or agreement |

### Avoid These Terms
- Unexplained acronyms on SMB pages (RAID, UAT, FRD are fine for FI buyers only)
- "Solutions", "synergy", "leverage" (as a verb), "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "world-class"
- "We" for the company or the work. Write "I". Inclusive "we" (you and me) is fine.

### Never-Use Terms
- Client names (YNCU, Sunrise, or any current or former client) in public copy (AGENTS.md rule 5)
- The current client's name or the existence of the agreement, in any outreach
- Fabricated metrics, testimonials or anecdotes

## Language That Works

### Top Phrases (from shipped copy)
1. "Delivery, not decks."
2. "Bridging the gap between digital strategy and platform execution."
3. "I don't just advise from the sidelines."
4. "So nothing falls through the cracks."
5. "Internal capability, not dependency."
6. "I stay involved until the platform is running smoothly and your team is confident."
7. "From first call to production." / "From first call to working assistant."
8. "You review and send." (SMB: keeps the human in control)

### Questions That Engage
- "Have a digital banking project in mind?"
- "What takes up most of your week?"
- "How many hours could you get back?"
- "Three questions every credit union should ask before a Backbase migration" (LinkedIn)

### Objection Handling Patterns
- **"I'm not technical."** → "No. If you can use email and a phone app, you can use this."
- **"Is my data safe?"** → "Your email and files stay in your own accounts."
- **"What's the catch on cost?"** → State the ongoing cost plainly ("Just the AI subscription").
- **"Will we depend on you forever?"** → "I help your team build internal capability, not dependency."

---

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns
- **Faceless-firm voice:** "Our team of experts delivers end-to-end solutions." Rewrite in "I" with a concrete verb.
- **Noun-stacking without verbs:** the content brief asks for "verbs, not nouns".
- **AI hype:** "Revolutionize your business with AI." The brand promises conservative, explained estimates.
- **Over-claiming scale:** don't imply a team or firm capacity that doesn't exist.
- **Salesy outreach:** pitching in a first note, adding links, a second follow-up.

## Content Examples

### Excellent Examples
> "I don't just advise from the sidelines. I configure, I test, I sit in the room with your team during UAT. Delivery, not decks." (WhatIBring)

*Why:* first person, three concrete verbs, short sentences, a memorable closing contrast.

> "I help credit unions and banks migrate to modern digital banking platforms, managing the governance, scope, and vendor coordination that determines whether a project lands on time or derails. From Discovery through production cutover, I own the delivery." (Services)

*Why:* names the audience, the hard part, and the accountability.

> "Long threads summarized, urgent items flagged, replies drafted in your voice. You review and send." (`/ai-savings`)

*Why:* plain language, a concrete benefit, the human stays in control.

### Examples to Avoid
> "We help financial services organizations modernize their digital channels, implement enterprise platforms, and build products that drive real business outcomes." (original content brief hero, since replaced)

*Why:* "we" plus generic nouns. "Real business outcomes" says nothing. It could be any firm.

## Decisions (formerly Open Questions)

### Resolved (decided 2026-11-15)

1. **"I" vs "we"** → **"I" everywhere.** "We" only in the inclusive you-and-me sense and in legal pages (Privacy, Terms). The stray "We look at your week" on `/ai-savings` is fixed.
2. **Years of experience** → **"15+ years"** (career started 2010). The site, the content brief and the Operations brand spec now match.
3. **Primary tagline** → headline: **"Launch your digital banking platform."** Core message: **"Bridging the gap between digital strategy and platform execution."** Page title: **"Adanac Alpine Advisory — Digital Banking Platform Delivery"** ("Digital enablement…" is retired). The footer keeps **"Elevated Technology Consulting — British Columbia"** by choice.
4. **Company promotion on LinkedIn** → **No.** Per the agreement with the former employer, no promotional Adanac Alpine content on LinkedIn.
5. **SMB AI offer** → **one brand.** It stays under Adanac Alpine with the same voice and the plain-language rule for SMB readers.
6. **Contact address** → `hello@` on public pages, the personal address in 1:1 messages.
7. **"Strategic partnership, not just vendor relationship"** → **retired.** Use "Delivery, not decks."
8. **Language** → **English only.** Speaking Russian appears only as a skill on the "Bilingual" card in What I Bring. No content is produced in Russian.
9. **Spelling** → **Canadian** ("favour", "centre", "colour"), keeping -ize endings ("optimize"). Current site copy already complies.

No open questions remain.
